Context

About Pickcel & the Design Challenge

Pickcel is a cloud-based digital signage SaaS platform that enables businesses to create, schedule, and manage content across thousands of screens — from retail stores and corporate lobbies to hospitals and transport hubs.

Despite a powerful product, the marketing website had evolved organically over time — sections added piecemeal, messaging updated without restructuring. The result was a homepage that overwhelmed rather than guided.

"The old website tried to explain everything. The new one is designed to make someone book a demo in under 3 minutes."

— Design rationale, Karthikeyan

The brief: communicate product value faster, guide buyers through a clear decision journey, and build trust progressively — not just at the bottom of the page.

Industry

Digital Signage SaaS — B2B, serving retail, enterprise, hospitality, healthcare

Primary Users

Marketing managers, IT teams, and operations leads evaluating signage platforms

Core Problem

High bounce rate, unclear value communication, low demo-booking conversion

Design Decisions

Section-by-Section Breakdown

Each section of the page was redesigned with a specific job. Here's what changed, why, and the UX principle behind it.

01

Hero — Product-Forward Visual, Outcome-Based Headline

Old hero: lifestyle photo, no product UI, feature-description headline. New hero: real signage screens in action, audience-directed headline "Manage Your Digital Screens. Captivate Every Audience." with dual CTAs for different buyer readiness levels.

Why it matters: B2B visitors make a value-fit judgment in 5–8 seconds. A product-forward hero with outcome messaging reduces bounce dramatically.

02

Stats Bar — Context and Weight Added

Old: unlabelled row "5500+, 100,000+, 30+" — no context. New: 160k+ screens, 9,000+ businesses, 100+ countries, 70+ integrations — each with a descriptor. Transforms abstract numbers into proof of scale.

Why it matters: Unlabelled numbers are ambiguous. Labelling precisely is the difference between "that sounds big" and "that means something."

Design Principles

UX Principles That Guided Decisions

🎯

Jobs-to-be-Done Messaging

Buyers don't want a "signage platform" — they want to captivate audiences and reduce manual updates. Every headline was rewritten to match the job, not the feature.

🧭

Buyer Journey Architecture

Hero → Stats → Onboarding → Features → Plan Selector → Dashboard → Testimonial → Integrations → Security → FAQ. Each section handed off to the next.

📐

Progressive Disclosure

Leads with outcomes before features. Product depth revealed gradually — complexity added only as visitor demonstrates interest by scrolling.

🤝

Distributed Trust Architecture

Trust signals distributed throughout: stats up top, testimonial mid-page, security in a dedicated section — not clustered at the bottom.

⚡

CTA Hierarchy and Timing

Primary CTAs only at hero and plan selector. Secondary links available for users needing more context. No CTA in every section.

🔍

Objection-First Sequencing

"Too complicated?" → Onboarding section. "Works with our tools?" → Integrations. "Is it secure?" → Security section. Each objection handled at the right scroll depth.

Reflection

What I Learned

01

B2B websites are persuasion documents, not product manuals

Moving from "show everything" to "show the right thing at the right moment" was the biggest shift. Comprehensive homepages lose visitors who just need to know if it's worth a 30-minute demo call.

02

Visual consistency is a trust signal in enterprise B2B

Enterprise procurement teams assess product quality through website quality. An inconsistent visual system creates doubt about reliability. Bringing typography, spacing, and colour into alignment was credibility management.

03

Self-segmentation removes sales friction before the sales team enters

The plan-selector was the most strategically valuable decision. When visitors self-identify their path, the website does qualification work that would otherwise require a sales touchpoint.

04

Competitive benchmarking should inform gaps, not directions

Auditing Screenly, Yodeck, NoviSign, and OnSign TV revealed where the category was underserving buyers — not what to copy. The annotated dashboard and distributed trust architecture were areas where Pickcel could genuinely lead.

05

Copy and design must be iterated together

Changing the headline required changes to layout, typographic treatment, and accompanying visual. Good redesigns don't separate these concerns — they're deeply interdependent.
