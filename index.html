Project Overview

What is SafeSource and who is it for?

SafeSource v3 is an internal B2B tool used by food manufacturer QA (Quality Assurance) teams to manage supplier compliance. It replaces email chains and spreadsheets with a structured, auditable system for tracking food safety certifications, sending formal queries to suppliers, and escalating compliance failures.

The primary user is Riya Kapoor, a QA Manager, who oversees 48 active suppliers across Dairy, Meat, Produce, and Packaging categories. Her core jobs-to-be-done are: spot compliance risk before it becomes a problem, send and track formal queries to suppliers, and escalate when suppliers don't respond.

The "v3" naming signals this is a mature, iterated product — the designer has gone through at least two earlier versions and refined based on feedback. This is a strong signal of real product thinking.

Full Case Study

Problem, process, decisions, and outcomes

The Problem

Food manufacturer QA teams operate in a high-stakes environment where a single expired supplier certificate can trigger a product recall, fail an audit, or create legal liability. Yet most QA managers manage supplier compliance through a patchwork of email, shared spreadsheets, and calendar reminders — a system that breaks when volume increases or team members change.

The core problem SafeSource solves: QA managers need to know which suppliers are at risk, right now, and take action without switching context.

Screens & What They Solve

Strong
Dashboard — Action-First Design
The dashboard leads with an "Action Banner" ("3 things need action before end of day") rather than passive data. This is excellent UX thinking — it respects the user's time and makes urgency visible immediately. The AI Smart Suggestion block with confidence score (94%) and reasoning panel shows mature thinking about how AI should communicate in high-stakes contexts.
Strong
Escalation Flags — Contextual, Not Just Alerting
Each escalation flag includes the "why" behind the severity rating (e.g. "Why high: 2 expired certs + no supplier response + audit grade C"). This is a key insight — most dashboards show alerts without reasoning, forcing users to investigate. SafeSource embeds the reasoning inline, reducing cognitive load and time to action.
Strong
Raise Query — 3-Step Progressive Disclosure
The query creation flow uses numbered steps (Supplier → Template → Details) with auto-populated fields (priority auto-set to High based on supplier risk profile, pre-filled subject line, pre-written message). This shows deep workflow knowledge — experienced QA managers want speed, not blank forms.
Strong
Query Detail — Communication + Audit Trail in One
The detail view combines a real-time chat thread with a structured activity log timeline. This is smart because it serves two different needs: the supplier-facing conversation (informal, fast) and the internal audit trail (formal, permanent). Most tools separate these; SafeSource merges them without losing clarity.
Gap
Supplier Risk Heatmap — Interaction Ambiguity
The heatmap cells use ✓, !, ✕ symbols (which is excellent for accessibility) but the interaction model is unclear — some cells have onclick handlers, others don't. There's no legend for what each row/column represents beyond the category label. A QA manager scanning this quickly may miss which specific supplier a cell belongs to.
Gap
Query Tracker — Column Density
The tracker table has 9 columns in a fixed grid. At standard laptop resolution this will feel tight. There's no column resizing, sorting indicators, or saved filter states. For a QA manager who lives in this view all day, these omissions add friction over time.
Missing
Empty States & Onboarding
No empty state designs visible for when there are 0 queries, 0 escalations, or 0 expired certificates. These moments are critical — a new user who logs in with no data needs guidance, not a blank screen. Also no first-run onboarding or tooltips for new QA managers joining the team.
Missing
Mobile / Tablet Breakpoints
The design is fully desktop-first. Given that QA managers often conduct supplier audits on-site with tablets, the lack of responsive breakpoints is a meaningful gap. The supplier table grid (9 columns) would be unusable on any screen under 1100px wide.
Key Design Decision Worth Discussing in Interview: The AI confidence score (94%) and the inline "Why this suggestion?" reasoning panel shows the designer understood a critical principle — AI in high-stakes workflows must be explainable, not just authoritative. This is a sophisticated insight that shows product maturity.

UX Focus Areas

The 5 most important UX principles at work in this design

🎯
Focus Area 1 — Attention Economy & Priority Signalling
The design correctly treats the user's attention as the scarcest resource. The Action Banner, AI Suggestion, and Escalation Flags are all above the fold and use a consistent visual hierarchy: red = act now, amber = act soon, green = okay. The designer understood that in a compliance tool, missed signals have real consequences — so the UI does the prioritisation work so the human doesn't have to.
🤖
Focus Area 2 — Human-AI Collaboration Design
The AI box is one of the most sophisticated elements in the design. It shows: a suggestion, the reasoning behind it, a confidence score, estimated impact, time to act, and risk if ignored. It then offers a "Dismiss" button. This reflects an understanding that AI in high-stakes tools must be assistive, not authoritative — the human must remain in control and trust must be earned through transparency.
📋
Focus Area 3 — Progressive Disclosure in Complex Forms
The Raise Query screen uses 3 numbered steps to break down a multi-field form. More importantly, it uses context-awareness to pre-fill fields (priority auto-set, subject auto-generated, message pre-written). This reduces the user's cognitive load from "what do I write?" to "does this look right?" — a fundamentally different and faster interaction model.
📊
Focus Area 4 — Data Density vs. Clarity Balance
The supplier table packs 9 columns of data while remaining readable — achieved through careful typographic hierarchy (supplier name 600 weight, metadata 400 weight), meaningful use of color-only as a secondary signal (primary signal is text/symbol), and micro-details like the 4px left border colour bar indicating risk level at a glance. This is data-dense design done with intention.
♿
Focus Area 5 — Non-Color Risk Communication
A subtle but important accessibility decision: risk chips use CSS ::before pseudo-elements to prepend triangle (▲), circle (●), and down-triangle (▼) symbols before the color-coded text. This ensures risk levels are communicated even in grayscale or for users with color vision deficiency. The heatmap also uses ✓, !, ✕ symbols rather than relying on color alone. This shows genuine accessibility thinking, not just color contrast checking.

Opportunities for v4

Specific, actionable improvements to discuss in interview

🔴 High Priority
Kanban View for Query Tracker
Replace the 9-column table with a Kanban board (columns: New → Awaiting Supplier → Awaiting Review → Resolved). This maps to how QA managers mentally think about work in progress and reduces scan time dramatically.
🔴 High Priority
Draft Auto-Save in Raise Query
If a user accidentally navigates away from the Raise Query form, all work is lost. Auto-save to local draft with a "You have a draft" banner on return would prevent frustration and data loss.
🟡 Medium Priority
Error States & Validation Design
Design explicit error states for: form validation, file upload failure, network timeout, supplier not found. Each should have a clear message, an icon, and a recovery action — not just a red border.
🟡 Medium Priority
Responsive Tablet Layout
QA managers conduct on-site audits with iPads. A tablet-optimised layout would: collapse the supplier table to show 4 key columns, make the heatmap cells 44px touch targets, and simplify the Raise Query form to a wizard-style flow.
🟢 Enhancement
Certificate Expiry Calendar View
A monthly calendar showing upcoming certificate expirations across all 48 suppliers would let QA managers do proactive planning ("3 certs expire next month — time to run bulk outreach"). This would reduce the reactive firefighting pattern visible in the dashboard.
🟢 Enhancement
Supplier Self-Service Portal
The current design is entirely internal. A supplier-facing view where suppliers can upload certificates, view outstanding queries, and update their contact details would reduce QA manager workload and improve data quality. This is a natural v4 scope expansion.
